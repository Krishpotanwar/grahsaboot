# R3 science and ML research: change detection, derived datasets, foundation models

Access date for every URL below: **2026-10-05**. Author: R3 science worker (Sonnet). Scope: the science parts of `research/2026-10-05-science-audit.md`, `ARCHITECTURE_V2.md` and `sources/SOURCE_TEXT.md` (the student proposal), plus a hunt for newer options.

Conventions. FACT = read from a primary source today. INFERENCE = my reasoning from facts. NOT RUN = no imagery was processed and no model was trained by me; the sandbox has no numpy, GDAL or rasterio, so no AEF raster was sampled. Where a page was blocked or only a secondary summary was available, the row says so.

## 0. Headline findings (read this first)

1. **Every ML ingredient of the student proposal is non-commercial or unlicensed, not just LEVIR-CD.** The OSCD change labels are **CC BY-NC-SA** ("For commercial purposes, please contact the authors"), per the dataset's own README as served by IEEE DataPort. The BIT code repo says "non-commercial and research purposes only". The FC-Siam reference repo has no licence file. The existing audit left OSCD "unverified" because DataPort was blocked; it is now resolved, and the answer is worse than "unknown". (R3-06, R3-07, R3-08)
2. **The F1 >= 0.55 target is the 2018 baseline's own number.** Daudt et al. report FC-Siam-diff at F1 57.92 on OSCD with 13 bands and 48.86 with 3 bands. Pretrained foundation-model backbones fine-tuned on OSCD land at 51.7 to 54.3 in SpectralGPT's table (a different protocol). So 0.55 is neither ambitious nor evidence of anything about verdict accuracy. (R3-01, R3-02)
3. **A newer "automatic assistance" option exists and is genuinely cheap: Google's AlphaEarth Satellite Embedding, 2017 to 2025, CC BY 4.0, free anonymous COGs on AWS via Source Cooperative, no Earth Engine needed.** But it is annual (one vector per pixel per calendar year), and a September 2026 independent paper states annual layers "are not yet validated for comparison over time". It is a validation-gated month-two experiment, not a month-one feature. (R3-N1)
4. **Dynamic World is Earth-Engine-only in the sources I could verify, so it is out for a commercial, zero-cash product.** Earth Engine's own terms put operational services under commercial licensing, and the pricing page lists paid plans. (R3-N3)
5. **Real, validated precedent for building-construction detection from free Sentinel data exists** (DLR zfv 2022: 34 of 38 new houses, Bavaria; Belgian SARSAR operational tool). It uses per-acquisition time series with simple thresholds, not siamese deep nets, and its thresholds were tuned on the same 38 sites. This supports the architecture's "real dated acquisitions, no composites, no ML at launch" stance. (R3-14)
6. **Road-specific validation from Sentinel-1/2 exists only for high-contrast tropical-forest roads** (Congo Basin F1 0.909; Amazon recall/precision 65%/71%). Nothing I found validates new-road detection against Indian rural roads, whose carriageway is 3.0 to 3.75 m inside a 7.5 m formation, i.e. less than one 10 m pixel. (R3-12, R3-13)

**What this means.** I found no foundation-model paper that shows validated bitemporal urban or construction change detection on Sentinel-2 beating a small supervised network. The only like-for-like data point is SpectralGPT's OSCD table: a random-initialised ViT reaches 51.71 F1 and the best pretrained model 54.29, i.e. about 2.6 points. INFERENCE: for a 10-site-per-state validation set that gain is far below measurement noise. Foundation models earn their place later as feature extractors for a small head, not as a month-one dependency.

CPU inference cost: I did not measure it. From parameter counts alone, Nano/Tiny OlmoEarth-class encoders are the only family where CPU serving of a per-site chip is plausible without a GPU; 300M to 600M Prithvi/TerraMind sizes would need batched or GPU serving (INFERENCE).

### 2.3 AlphaEarth for change detection: what is established and what is not

Established (FACT):
- Annual embeddings are consistent across years and designed for dot-product or angle comparisons (Earth Engine catalog description).
- Google itself ships a "Detect change" tool built on them, labelled experimental pre-GA under the Maps Platform Service Specific Terms, annual, 10 m; India availability is not stated. https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/earth/detect-change
- Independent Sept 2026 paper (arXiv 2609.30356): across cities, annual embedding paths run 11.45 degrees per year but retain only 1.91 degrees net displacement ("repeated directional reversal rather than steady drift"); monthly weather anomalies reduce error in predicting annual distance by 12.7% (17.0% with retrieval-quality data); a 4.1 percentage-point contraction appeared in cities losing Sentinel-1B passes; the authors state "annual layers are not yet validated for comparison over time". In eleven large construction projects (Paris Olympic Village, Cairo New Capital, Phoenix) the median excess displacement over matched controls was 20.2 degrees (range 7.8 to 57.5). https://arxiv.org/html/2609.30356v1
- 12-city slum study (2017 to 2024): best same-city cross-year spatial F1 0.616, with positive-pixel R² "consistently negative" at 10 m, and strong city-scale drift. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10029 (abstract read; the PDF text was garbled).

Not established (so gated):
- Detection of 500 m² to 1000 m² structures or sub-pixel roads in India.
- Any calibration of "distance" into a probability.
- Whether the annual product's inter-annual noise (weather, sensor loss) exceeds the signal for a 1 km² site.

INFERENCE: the only defensible use is **relative**: compare the site's year-over-year embedding distance against the distribution from matched surrounding pixels (a local control ring), as the Sept 2026 paper did with matched controls. An absolute threshold would be indefensible.

## 4. Decision: what automatic assistance is honest and shippable

### 4.1 Month one (ship)

1. **Selected real observations workbench as in Arch** (R3-13 supports it).
2. **One optional ML-free visual aid per comparison: a plainly labelled "spectral difference" view** between the two selected acquisitions (for example NIR/red/NDVI difference rendered by the same Sentinel Hub evalscript call that renders the images). No threshold, no polygon output, no label "construction". Caption: "Brightness/vegetation difference between these two dates. Weather, season and soil moisture also cause differences." This is the proposal's own "naive NDVI/NDBI floor" and costs no new infrastructure.
3. **Reference overlays only, behind a toggle**: PMGSY road alignment (after a human verifies the portal's licence text), OSM roads, and Open Buildings/Microsoft footprints, each with attribution and vintage. They answer "what is claimed or mapped here", not "what was built".
4. **Consent-based capture of user annotations as future labels**: the product's own dated, geometry-tied "change / no clear change / uncertain" notes become a licence-clean training and evaluation set. This is the only labelled data in the plan with a clear commercial right.

### 4.2 Needs a validation gate (month two or later; do offline first)

| Candidate | Why it may work | Gate (pre-register before looking at predictions) |
|---|---|---|
| **A. AlphaEarth annual distance "hotspot hint"**, relative to a matched control ring | Cheapest possible compute; open data; published positive evidence on 11 large projects | Offline Python, not production. At least 60 labelled AOIs split by project and region (not by patch), with positives and stable controls and including monsoon/agricultural confounders; report AUROC and precision at fixed alert rates with Wilson/bootstrap intervals, per size class (< 1000 m², 1000 to 10,000 m², roads). Pass only if the lower confidence bound beats the NDVI-difference baseline. Reviewers who label must not see the scores. |
| **B. Small supervised head on 64-D embedding differences** | Logistic regression or gradient-boosted trees on our own labels; CPU only | Same gate; train only on labels with a commercial right (own annotations, or OSCD after written permission). |
| **C. FC-Siam-diff reimplemented from the paper, Sentinel-2 chips** | Strong, cheap baseline per 2026 benchmark; licence-clean if rewritten | Same gate; this is the "scratch" comparator for A and B. |

Do not ship a "verdict" (verified / partial / stalled / not constructed) at any stage until a gate passes **per class** with grouped splits (audit SCI-21 to SCI-23).

### 4.3 Scale path for ML

1. Month 1: no ML; log annotations and observation provenance; keep product IDs and processing baselines (already in Arch).
2. Months 2 to 3: offline evaluation of A, B, C on the labelled set, executed on a laptop or an existing free notebook; AEF reads via range requests and the `.vrt` files.
3. If A/B pass: precompute a per-site AEF-distance layer at request time (a tiny read plus a dot product, no GPU), cached in storage. Cost scales with reads of ~640 kB per site-year, not with model size.
4. Only if a head plateaus: fine-tune a small foundation backbone (Clay Apache-2.0 or OlmoEarth Nano/Tiny after a licence review) on accumulated labels; training needs a GPU once, inference stays CPU-sized. Large Prithvi/TerraMind sizes are not needed for this task on current evidence.
5. At each step the abstain/insufficient-evidence outcome from the audit stays first-class.

## 5. Recommendation and the strongest argument against

**Recommendation.** Ship month one with no automatic detection: real dated acquisitions, an unlabelled-as-detection spectral-difference view, reference overlays, and a consent-based label capture. Treat AlphaEarth annual embedding distance (relative to a control ring) as the leading, gated experiment, evaluated offline from the free Source Cooperative COGs. Reject Dynamic World (Earth Engine only), treat OSCD, LEVIR-CD, BIT code and ICCD as non-commercial until written permission, and drop the F1 >= 0.55, "BIT is better for roads", "no published system" and "75% accuracy" statements from every document.

**Strongest argument against this pick.** Shipping no automatic change cue makes GeoVerify a thin viewer in a market where Sentinel Hub's EO Browser, Google Earth and Google's own "Detect change" already show comparisons, and users will ask "where exactly did it change?". Road corridors up to 10 km and polygons up to 1 km² are too large to scan reliably by eye, so the hint is the feature that earns return visits. AlphaEarth is free, CC BY 4.0, already on AWS, needs no GPU, and the Sept 2026 paper shows a 20.2 degree median excess over matched controls on eleven big projects, so a heatmap labelled "experimental, annual, not validated" would be cheap to add and arguably no less honest than an unlabelled difference view. Withholding it also delays the only data we would get on whether users trust or ignore such hints, and delays accumulating labels from real interaction.

**Why I still pick gating.** (1) The authors of the one independent critique say annual layers are not yet validated for temporal comparison, and the sample of projects behind the positive result is exactly the large, high-contrast kind our users with small sites do not have. (2) A 1 km² site is only about 100 x 100 pixels, where a hint adds little over looking at the images; the value concentrates in long corridors, the case with the least validation (section 3). (3) Automation bias: a red heatmap on a legal-adjacent evidence report is read as a finding, whatever the caption says. (4) An annual product cannot honour the architecture's date-interval contract ("first visible between observation A and B"). (5) Engineering: unofficial mirror, bottom-up COGs, Edge Function CPU limit. If the offline gate in 4.2 passes, promoting the hint is a small step; if it fails, we avoided publishing a plausible-looking map that was wrong.
